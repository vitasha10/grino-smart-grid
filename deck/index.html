SunGuard · one box per home

Helps from the very first home.

  1. Watches the voltage every second at your panel.
  2. Keeps your inverter running — switches on its own voltage control and eases power down instead of shutting down.
  3. On your phone — over home Wi-Fi: live voltage, alerts and a log for ENA.

On the 2,614 problem lines.

3

Why: a live map of problem lines without buying new meters.

Pays: for the data.

Alternatives today: the installer sets voltage control once (our first advice — where it is enough, no box is needed); the inverter’s app (sees one inverter, after it trips); a plain voltage relay (no control, no data).

What it took

24 hours, three pivots.

  • 7 ideas tested — and dropped, with reasonsbatteries, automatic line switching, phase balancer, selling noon surplus, dynamic tariffs, sensors in every substation, long-range radio — all listed on our research site
  • 820,000+ rows of the regulator’s data read → 2,614 problem lines found
  • Armenian law checked: radio bands, net metering, grid rules → the design changed twice (radio → Wi‑Fi)
  • A grid model and a calculator for 400 real lines, cross-checked to 0.1 V
  • Three pivots after mentor feedback: software for ENA → a box for problem streets → a box for every home, over Wi-Fi
  • The box designed with Yerevan prices: ≈ $30 of parts
